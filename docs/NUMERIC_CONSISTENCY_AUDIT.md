# Numeric consistency audit

Date: 2026-09-07. Read-only; no document other than this one was changed.

About 150 numeric claims were traced across 19 documents (the measurement design and standard, the coefficient audit, the evidence reclassification, the arm registry, the two post-decay-floor documents, the Ferret-to-Hamster handoff, plans 0087 to 0094, the two decision packets, the status summary, and the source reports those documents cite). Each claim was checked against the hash-bound record in `docs/experiments/`, and where one exists, against the run artifact under `ard-runtime/.../runs/`.

**14 claims failed.** All 14 are VERIFIED: both the claim and the record were read. Nine are material, meaning a decision that is still open could be misdirected by them. Five are footnotes. No finding is merely SUSPECTED; the few numbers that could not be traced to a record are listed separately and are not counted as failures.

The single most consequential finding is the first row of the table: the plan that preregisters the next GPU campaign (plan 0093) still attributes to its primary arm an effect that belongs to a different experiment, even though the same transcription was corrected in the handoff document the day before.

## Terms used below

- **pp** — percentage point. On the 5,000-image validation split one image is 0.02 pp; on the 10,000-image official test set one image is 0.01 pp.
- **Floor**, written **σ_d** — how far apart two training runs land when nothing differs between them except randomness. It is a standard deviation of a difference between two runs, in pp. An effect smaller than the floor cannot be told apart from nothing.
- **Pre-decay / post-decay** — before or after the learning-rate drop at epoch 100. The pre-decay floor is 1.14 to 1.25 pp. The post-decay floor was guessed at 0.25 to 0.50 pp (working value 0.40) until 2026-09-06, when plan 0092 measured it at 0.159 / 0.124 / 0.092 pp for epochs 104 / 109 / 114 (`docs/experiments/ard_post_decay_floor_v1.json`).
- **MDE** — minimum detectable effect: the smallest effect a design can find with 80% power at the 5% level. With k paired blocks and a known floor it is about 2.8 × σ_d / √k; with the floor estimated from four degrees of freedom the factor is 3.49 instead of 2.8.
- **Block / pair** — one treated run and one control run forked from the same parent checkpoint. Two seeds with one control each is k = 2.
- **Validation split vs official test** — the 5,000 held-out training images used for screens, versus the 10,000-image CIFAR-10 test set used once for confirmation.
- **CE-PGD20 vs AutoAttack** — the fixed 20-step attack used on every screen, versus the stronger attack ensemble used only on saved checkpoints for confirmation.
- **Horizon** — the epoch at which a number was measured: e84, e94 (pre-decay), e104, e109, e114, e199 (post-decay).

## Recurring failure patterns

Only patterns seen more than once are listed.

**1. The number was recalled, not copied.** Findings #1, #7 and #13. A memorable triple (+0.41 / +0.06 / +0.46) was written into a table for a two-seed arm; a control's whole-training-split accuracy was written into a field named "held-out"; a coefficient was typed with the wrong trailing digits. This happens when a human retypes a figure from memory of a recent document instead of from the record path. The one check that catches all three: **a number may appear in prose only next to the record path and key it was read from, and a value count that does not match the design's seed or block count is a hard stop.** The tell in #1 was visible without opening any file.

**2. A quantity measured in one regime was set beside a quantity from another, with the regime unnamed.** Findings #3, #4, #6, #9 (line 58) and #10. Pre-decay floors were quoted at post-decay endpoints; a validation CE-PGD20 floor was used to size an official-test AutoAttack endpoint; a training-log SD stood in for an endpoint SD; an e114 two-seed MDE was compared with an e199 three-seed effect. This happens because "the floor" and "the effect" are spoken of as single numbers when each is a family indexed by split, attack, horizon and design. The one check: **every floor or effect carries its four labels (split, attack, horizon, design) in the same cell, and two numbers may be compared only when all four match.** The measurement standard's own naming rule does this for arms; it needs the same rule for measurements.

**3. A provisional number outlived the measurement that replaced it.** Findings #8, #9, #14, and the stale halves of #3 and #6. The 0.25-0.50 bracket, the 0.40 working value and the 0.35 provisional σ_d were each marked provisional where they were introduced, and each was left in place after 2026-09-06 22:59. The handoff even instructed the reader to replace 0.35 (line 64) and then used it (lines 170-172). This happens because the record import closes the plan that produced the number but nothing enumerates the documents that consumed the old one. The one check: **at record import, grep the repository for the superseded literals (`0.35 pp`, `0.25–0.50`, `0.40 pp`, `never measured`, `暫定`) and list every hit in the postrun report; plan 0092's open milestone M4 is exactly this step and should not be optional.**

**4. One name was used for two different objects, and the two were then pooled or counted together.** Findings #2, #5 and #11. "CROPSHIFT" named both a 200-epoch CropShift run and a CropShift-then-RandomErasing run, and the five differences were averaged; "plan 0093" was assigned the power of the two-block screen; "17 post-decay rows" was stated three times without anyone re-counting the column. This happens when a short label is carried across documents and the thing it points to changes underneath it. The one check: **an effect is always written with its comparator spelled out in canonical form (for example `I100 − Aug(CropShift→CROP_RE@e100)`), and differences whose comparators are not identical are never averaged.** The arm registry already has the canonical names; the five-seed report and the reclassification's row A2 did not use them.

Sources: `docs/ARM_REGISTRY.md:94, 97, 99, 100` defines `CROP_SUFFIX` as
`Aug(CropShift->CROP_RE@e100)` and `CROPSHIFT` as `Aug(CropShift) @all`;
`docs/ERT_RSLAD_UNSEEN_CONFIRMATION_RESULTS.md:51` gives the confirmation
figures directly; `docs/ERT_RSLAD_FIVE_SEED_STOCHASTICITY.md` reports all five
under one name.

The two groups do not overlap at all — the largest confirmation value, 0.78 pp,
is below the smallest development value, 1.04 pp — while the spread inside each
group is about 0.08 pp.  A gap that clean between groups whose members agree
that closely is not sampling noise.  It is what a stronger comparator looks
like: the confirmation control also receives a late augmentation change, so
there is less left for the treatment to add.

**What this does and does not do to the I100 result.**  It does not overturn it.
Both contrasts are strongly positive, and split apart each is *more* significant
than the pooled figure, not less.  What it removes is the headline: `+0.864 pp`
with `t(4) = 7.82` describes a quantity that does not exist, because the five
measurements are of two different questions —

- "does switching to IDBH_WEAK at epoch 100 beat not switching?"  `+1.12 pp`
- "does switching to IDBH_WEAK at epoch 100 beat switching to CROP_RE?"  `+0.69 pp`

Both deserve reporting.  Neither should be reported as the other, and their
average is not a finding.  Anything downstream that used `+0.864 pp` as a single
effect — including the 0.25 pp "type (c)" floor derived from this contrast —
needs revisiting against whichever of the two questions it actually meant.
